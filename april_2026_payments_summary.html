The Calendar Unwind
2.1 ARR · 12-month trend

ARR snapped back to the February high — the calendar effect cleanly unwound.

February's compressed cycle pulled ARR forward. March gave it back. April closed the loop at $15.49M, equal to the Feb peak. Net of the calendar swing, the underlying engine added ~+5K paid leases / month Feb→Apr — that's real momentum, not just a rebound.

2.2 Paid Leases · the milestone

48.06% paid — record, but ~0.5pp is mechanical.

The +1.13pp MoM lift was conversion-led: unpaid → paid hit 9.4% (vs 7.8% baseline) and delivered +0.45pp on its own — more than retention, churn, and new-active combined.

The asterisk: ~1.7K of the gain is mechanical reactivation, not engine output. Leases that pre-paid in February's compressed cycle (calendar carryover) skipped March and resumed in April — landed in the "Reactivated" bucket but aren't real recovery. Without that lift, paid % sits at ~47.6% — still an all-time high, just less dramatic than 48.06%.

Structural Drift · Card

Adoption is the cause · share is the symptom.

Card share fell to a 12-month low (17.75%) even though absolute card volume hit a record ($54.66M). The drift is being driven by each new cohort entering with less card — a pattern visible for several months: first-time-tenant card adoption has fallen ~9pp since Mar '25 (38.32% → 29.19%), while returning-tenant adoption has held flat near 27%. Stayer drift has been a steady ~0.65pp/month for 15 months — that's the constant headwind, not the mover. What's changed is the refill: each cohort is doing less to offset it.

Methodology · KPI 1 & KPI 2
Why the funnel uses a 45-day window

Of leases that eventually pay, 89.6% pay within 45 days of lease activation. Going to 60 days adds 4.2pp; everything past 90 days is <2.5pp of the signal. A 45-day cutoff captures almost all the signal while keeping the read fast.

Churned Accounts

PM-account churn fell sharply — first material relief in 7 months.

Scope: only accounts that received payments (payments ARR > 0) before churning — pure churn-of-paying-accounts, not total churn. Climbed from Sep '25 (67 accts / $77K) to a March peak of 127 / $244K. April fell to 90 / $125K — biggest single-month drop since Sep '25. Tracked on subscription end-date, so not a calendar artifact. YoY flat (88 → 90); ARR per churned account stable at ~$1.4K.

How "Lost Payments ARR" is calculated: for each churned account, take the max monthly payments ARR observed in the 6 months before its churn date. That's the recurring revenue that walks out the door. Sum across accounts that churned in the month.
